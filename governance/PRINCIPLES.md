# Principles

These principles are the constitution of goPrivate. Features, RFCs, and implementations should be judged against them.

1. **Privacy before convenience.** Convenience that weakens privacy is rejected.
2. **The protocol is open.** Anyone may implement interoperable clients and relays.
3. **Self-hosting is a first-class deployment.** Public hosted instances are optional; running your own stack must remain practical.
4. **No vendor lock-in.** No proprietary APIs, storage backends, or paid “enterprise” forks required for core use.
5. **Users own their conversations.** No accounts, no server-side history, no surveillance by design.
6. **The relay knows as little as possible.** After handshake, the relay forwards opaque ciphertext only.
7. **Simplicity over unnecessary features.** Prefer a small, auditable surface to feature sprawl.
8. **Security through open review.** Threat models, RFCs, and ADRs are public; secrecy is not a security strategy.
9. **Protocol first, application second.** Success is measured by protocol adoption, not by a single app’s popularity.
